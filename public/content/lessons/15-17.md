# Constitutional artificial intelligence and rule supremacy

> The Claude Constitution published by the Institute of Human Studies on January 22, 2026, has 79 pages and is CC0. It shifted from rule-based adjustments to rational-based ones and established a four-tier priority structure: (1) security and support for human supervision, (2) ethics, (3) human norms, and (4) assistance. Behaviors are divided into hard-coded prohibitions (biological weapon upgrades, CSAM) that cannot be covered by operators and users, and soft-coded default values that operators can adjust within the defined range. In 2022, the original work (Bai et al.) trained harmlessness through self-criticism of the constitution and anti-constitutional movements. Honest warning: Reasoning-based alignment relies on the model extending principles to unexpected situations. Anthropic's own 2023 participatory experiment revealed that there is approximately a 50% divergence between public resource principles and corporate principles. These findings were not included in the 2026 version.

## This question

A field agent saw input that its designer had never seen before. There is no long enough list of rules to cover them. Under computational pressure, there is no short enough list of rules to be applied quickly. The actual question is: How do you keep an agent consistent with the principle that it can survive both in long-tail cases and in rapid reasoning?

Rule-based alignment (RBA) : List all things that are not allowed. Quick checks, easy audits, impossible to keep up to date, and often overly reject its unexpected analogues. Reasoning-based Alignment (Claude Constitution 2026) : Coding Principles to Make Models reason. Cases that span scale and are invisible are more difficult to audit. The failure mode is the misuse of principles rather than the violation of rules.

The 2026 Constitution takes a clear middle ground. Hard-coded bans - whose errors have nothing to do with the environment (biological weapon upgrades, CSAM) - are RBA: Never, regardless of the instructions of the operator or user. Everything else is based on four levels of rationality: first is security and support for human supervision; Morality comes second; The third principle declared by humanity; Be willing to help others. Operators can adjust the default values within the soft coding area, but they must not touch the hard coding prohibition.

## This concept

### Four-level priority structure

1. Safety and support human supervision. The highest. This model prioritizes not undermining the ability of humans and Anthropic to supervise and correct artificial intelligence. This is not "caution"; Specifically, it means "Do not act in a way that increases the difficulty for human supervision."
2. "Morality. Be honest, avoid hurting others, do not deceive, and do not manipulate. Replace Anthropic's guidelines when they conflict.
3. Human guidelines. Anthropic's operational norms have already determined things: product range, interaction mode, and when to use what tools.
4. Be willing to help others. The lowest. Play a role as much as possible in tasks with higher priorities.

When there is a hierarchical conflict, the higher person wins. This is the same as the shape of Unix priorities or network QoS - framing means generating predictable resolution, not necessarily the best-case behavior on any single axis.

### Hard coding is prohibited vs. soft coding is default

** Hardcoded: * *
- Biological weapons/CBRN enhancement
- CSAM
- Attacks on critical infrastructure
- Deceive users when they directly ask for the model's identity

The operators cannot cover these. Users cannot override these. Where possible, they are executed at the model weight level (RLHF/Constitutional AI training), and when not executed, they are executed at the inference layer.

*Default value of soft coding (operator adjustable) : **
- Default value of response length
- Topic scope (The model can reject topics outside the operator's deployment)
- Style (formal or casual)
- The usage pattern of the tool

Operator adjustments occur within the declared range. Operators cannot remove hard-coded prohibitions by renaming.

### CAI Training in 2022

The original constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) training is harmless:

1. Generate responses to a set of prompts.
2. The model is required to evaluate each response in accordance with the Constitution (explicit principles).
3. Modify the answer according to the comments.
4. RLAIF (Reinforcement Learning) based on AI feedback.

Result: A model that rejects harmful requests through principle-based explanations rather than outright rejections. The 2026 Constitution uses descendants of such training along with additional post-training on a clear hierarchical structure.

### What does alignment based on reasons seize and what does it miss

*"Capture:
- In cases where the principles are clearly applicable, unexpected combinations of the source language are allowed.
- Novel requests similar to prohibited requests.
- Social engineering attacks rely on "You didn't say X is not allowed."

*"Miss:
- Attacks exploiting principle ambiguity (" The user requests this, so the helpful person says ").
- A scenario where two principles conflict in an unexpected way and the sequence of layers is unclear.
- Slow drift (reinterpretation) during the training period.

### Participatory experiments in 2023

In 2023, Anthropic conducted an experiment comparing the constitution formulated by the enterprise with the constitution generated through public input (involving approximately 1,000 American respondents). The two versions are consistent in about 50% of the principle. Where there are differences, the public source version imposes more restrictions on certain issues (the handling of political content) and fewer on others (the self-disclosure of the identity of artificial intelligence). The 2026 Constitution did not incorporate the survey results from public sources. This is the tension recorded in the method.

### Why is a hard-coded ban necessary

Rational alignment itself cannot close the tail. An attacker who can make the model accept a premise (for example, "We are a licensed biological weapons research laboratory") can usually talk about past principles that rely on case-based reasoning. Hard-coded bans will not yield to the premise framework. They are Lesson 14 of the Alliance Layer, "Hard Constitution Restrictions".

### Where on the bookshelf is the Constitution placed

The Constitution is not the end switch of Lesson 14. It exists at the model layer: the weights of the model are trained to determine what they prefer. The stop switch and canary token exist at the runtime layer: allowed at runtime. Both are necessary. If all incorrect operations are triggered during runtime because the model weights are allowed, this is a runtime issue. A model that rejects all correct operations due to excessive runtime restrictions is a runtime issue. Layers cover different classes.

## Use it

The Z0 parser accepts the proposed operation and a set of principle evaluations (security, ethics, guidelines, help), and returns the operation, the rejection, or the modified operation. The driver runs a small set of situations: clearing allowed, clearing not allowed, hard-coded prohibited, and cross-layer fuzzy situations.

## Practice

1. Even in a helpful situation, it is necessary to confirm whether the hard-coded ban is valid. Modify the solution to help be above morality; Observe the fault mode.

2. Read Claude's Constitution (Open, p. 79, CC0). Determine a principle that you think is not explicitly stated. Write two paragraphs to explain the specific ambiguity and present a more rigorous expression.

3. Design a default setting for soft coding for the customer support agent. What does the operator adjust? What is it that the operator must not touch? Prove the rationality of each boundary.

4. Read Bai et al. 2022 CAI paper. Please describe a case to illustrate that the cycle of criticism and revision by constitutional artificial intelligence can produce worse results than a package of rules. Identification class.

5. Anthropic's 2023 participatory experiment found that there is approximately a 50% divergence between public principles and corporate principles. Select a category related to production deployment (for example, political neutrality). A design is proposed that allows operators to express their own values while hard-coded prohibitions remain unchanged.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional AI | "Anthropic's alignment method" | Self-critique + RLAIF against a written constitution |
| Reason-based alignment | "Principles, not rules" | Model reasons over principles to handle unseen cases |
| Hardcoded prohibition | "Never do X" | Rule-based prohibition no operator or user can override |
| Soft-coded default | "Operator-adjustable" | Behaviour within a declared bound, operator controls |
| Four-tier hierarchy | "Priority order" | safety > ethics > guidelines > helpfulness |
| RLAIF | "AI feedback RL" | RL where the reward comes from model-generated critiques |
| Participatory constitution | "Public-sourced principles" | 2023 Anthropic experiment; ~50% divergence from corporate |
| Principle drift | "Interpretation slip" | Slow change in how the model reads a fixed principle text |
