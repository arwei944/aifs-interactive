### Why does the OG-narrator win

"Measurement of the Bargaining Power of Language Models" (arXiv:2402.15813) observed that:

- Master of Laws often violates bargaining rules (quoting absurd prices and ignoring the other party's ZOPA).
- They anchored very poorly (accepting a bad first offer; bargaining with symbolic rather than strategic amounts).
- Scale alone cannot solve these problems. Larger models use similar strategic errors to produce more credible language.

### NegotiationArena discovered

arXiv:2402.05863 provides the specification benchmark. Title Discovery

- A master of Laws can increase profits by 20% by using character manipulation (" I'm eager to sell this by Friday ") - character manipulation is a true strategy.
- Fair/cooperative agents are exploited by hostile agents; Defense requires a clear confrontational stance.
- Symmetrical pairing converges to unfair results in approximately 40% of the benchmark scenarios.

This is not "a master of Laws is a bad negotiator." "The negotiation of a master of Laws is too human-like, including the available parts." "

### The strings of thoughts are hidden

The Massive Autonomous Negotiation Contest (arXiv:2503.06416) ran approximately 180,000 negotiations in many LLM strategies. The winners concealed their reasoning from their peers:

- If the agent prints "I'm only selling for 75 dollars; My reservation price was 70 dollars, and the other party wrote it down on a publicly visible notepad.
- The winner calculates the strategy privately; The output channel only contains the quotation and the description of the minimum requirements.

This is the echo of the classic game theory of 2026 (Aumann's 1976 on reason and Information) : Revealing your private Valuation cost-return. Master of Laws do not intuitively know this and are willing to input their reservations into the reasoning traces visible to their peers.

Engineering insight: Separate the private scraper context from the public message context. It's not optional.

### Describe the mechanism rules

Among all the negotiation benchmarks for 2024-2026, the consistent engineering rule is:

> Let a master of Laws explain. Don't let the Master of Laws calculate the offer.

If the offer needs to be a number (price, estimated arrival time, quantity), generate it from the negotiation status and let the LLM generate the framework. If the offer needs to be a proposal structure (task breakdown, role allocation), let the LLM draft it, but verify it against patterns and constraint checks before sending.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Contract Net | "Task market" | Smith 1980, FIPA 1996. cfp + propose + accept/reject. The canonical task-market. |
| ZOPA | "Zone of possible agreement" | Overlap between buyer's max and seller's min. Offers outside it cannot close. |
| BATNA | "Best alternative to a negotiated agreement" | Your fallback if this deal fails. Sets your reservation price. |
| OG-Narrator | "Offer generator + narrator" | Decomposition: deterministic offer, LLM narration. |
| Zeuthen strategy | "Risk-minimizing concession" | Classical offer-generator that concedes based on risk limits. |
| Rubinstein bargaining | "Alternating-offer equilibrium" | Game-theoretic model for infinite-horizon bargaining with discounting. |
| CoT concealment | "Hide your reasoning" | Winners in arXiv:2503.06416 kept private scratchpads; public channel shows offer only. |
| Persona manipulation | "Emotional posturing" | arXiv:2402.05863: ~20% payoff gain from desperation/urgency personas. |
