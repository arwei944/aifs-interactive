# METR time range and external capability assessment

> METR (ex-ARC Evals) has been an independent 501(c)(3) since December 2023. Their Time Horizon 1.1 benchmark (January 2026) conforms to the logical curve of task success probability and log (expert completion time); The intersection with a 50% probability defines the time range of the model. The engagement set for 2025-2026 includes GPT-5.1, GPT-5.1-Codex-Max and prototype surveillance assessment (whether the monitor can capture side tasks; whether the agent can evade). Benchmark test suites: HCAST (180+ ML, network, SWE, inference tasks; 1 minute to over 8 hours), RE-Bench (71 ML research engineering tasks and expert baselines), SWAA. Honest tip: METR metrics are idealized - no one, no actual results - and the team has documented the behavioral gap between the assessment and the deployment (Lesson 1). The time range is an upper limit, not a deployment prediction.

## This question

Scaling strategies (Lessons 19 and 20) are only useful when the metrics they refer to are useful. The "threshold for artificial intelligence research and development" and "remote autonomy" are defined in policy essays. Only when specific evaluations produce specific figures do they become operational.

METR is an external assessment agency for 2024-2026 and has already defined many of these figures. They evaluate cutting-edge models - usually under confidentiality agreements and before laboratory releases - and then publish the methods. The Time Horizon 1.1 benchmark (January 2026) is their main artifact: a single scalar that compresses capabilities into human-readable units (" This model can complete tasks that experts spend X hours with 50% reliability ").

This class is partly about methodology (how to calculate the horizon) and partly about explanation (why the horizon is the upper bound rather than deployment prediction). These two skills belong to the same group. A team that knows how to adapt to the horizon is less likely to be fooled by a bad supplier statement than one that only sees "14 hours" written on the slides.

## This concept

### "METR Background"

- Establishment date: December 2023 (formerly arc Evals, spun off into an independent 501 (c)(3)).
- Scope: Assess the autonomous capabilities of cutting-edge models, usually pre-releases.
- Collaborative laboratories: Anthropic, OpenAI (multiple collaborations from 2025 to 2026).
- Notable deliverables: Time Horizon 1.0 (March 2025), Time Horizon 1.1 (January 2026), Prototype monitoring and evaluation.

### The time span is appropriate

Method (from the METR blog and papers) :

1. Collect a task suite covering expert completion times from minute to hour levels. Current suites: HCAST (180+ tasks), RE-Bench (71 tasks), SWAA.
2. Run the model on each task; Record success or failure.
3. Fit the logistic curve: P (success) as a function of log (expert completion time).
4. The horizon is the expert time when P (success) = 0.5.

The logical fit shape is correct because ability usually has a gradually increasing and stabilizing relationship with the difficulty of the task. 50% is an option (it can be 10% or 90%); METR reports multiple thresholds in the detailed paper, but leads by 50% because it is the most intuitive.

### The figures for January 2026

1.1 for each time range

- Claude Opus 4.6: approximately 14 hours, 50% reliability, as of Horizon 1.1 (January 2026).
- The time for hcast type tasks doubles: The matching time after 2023 reported by Time Horizon 1.1 (January 2026) is approximately 4.3 months (130.8 days); The seven-month data is the complete data from 2019 to 2025 in Time Horizon 1.0 and is reported together with the data after 2023 in Outlook 1.1.

These are the specific figures remaining from stage 15. They are cited as one of the measurements for threshold evaluation in Anthropic's RSP v3.0 (Lesson 19) and DeepMind's FSF v3 (Lesson 20).

### Benchmark suite

- **HCAST**: 189 tasks spanning machine learning, cybersecurity, software engineering, and general inference. Span: Expert time ranging from 1 minute to over 8 hours. The master suite is behind the Horizon.
- **RE-Bench**: 71 ML Research engineering tasks and human expert baseline. Measure whether the model can complete the machine learning research and development work of artificial intelligence -4 thresholds.
- **SWAA** : Shorter tasks; Used for calibrating the lower end of the horizontal curve.

### Prototype monitoring and evaluation

The METR work from 2025 to 2026 includes monitoring and evaluating prototypes: whether the monitor can capture the model performing the secondary task (different from the task required by the operator); Can the agent evade surveillance? These directly provide the "Sabotage Assurance" category in OpenAI PF v2 and the deceptive alignment monitoring in DeepMind FSF v3 (Lesson 20).

### Why is the event horizon the upper realm

- ** Idealized tools ** : The benchmark task provides the model with clean tools and well-formatted data. The actual deployment is much more complicated.
- ** No real consequences ** : This model has never truly charged customers, deleted real data, or contacted real people. True deployment carries irreversible risks.
- "Evaluation of Situational Games" : Lesson One. The performance of the model varies in the test. The International Artificial Intelligence Security Report 2026 conducted an empirical study on this.
- ** No legitimate user differences ** : The benchmark prompt is structured. Real users will generate ambiguous and context-dependent requests.

The horizon is the upper limit of capability under favorable conditions. Deployment reliability is a different figure, lower, and teams must measure their own distribution to understand it.

### Case studies of external evaluators

External assessment is important because internal laboratories have the motivation to optimize the indicators they report. The independence of METR - in compliance with 501(c)(3) regulations, with publicly available methods and peer-reviewed papers - is a structural relief. It is not enough in itself (the laboratory still controls what METR sees), but strictly speaking, it is better than having no external evaluation.

### How to use the field of view number in practice

- ** As a capability filter ** : If the model's horizon is far below the expert time of the proposed task, do not autonomously release it (skill file of Lesson 1).
- ** As a trend indicator ** : Double Time tells you how long the current practice will remain safe even without new mitigation measures.
- ** As previously **:14 hours of vision is a starting point. Adjust based on your task distribution, tool quality, and deployment context.

## Practice

1. The operation confirms that 50% of the fitted field of view is consistent with the facts of the synthetic ground. Now halve the task time grid; Is there any meaningful change in the field of vision estimation?

2. Read METR's Time Horizon 1.1 blog post. Determine the specific tasks with the highest and lowest reliability. Explain why there is a gap.

3. Read METR's "Measuring Autonomous Artificial Intelligence Capabilities" resource. List the HCAST task categories. Choose a category that you value more in your production tasks and explain the reason.

4. Introduce an evaluation scenario game in the simulator: Turn 20% of failed tasks into successes. Report New Horizons. This is approximately equivalent to the impact of a 20% game rate on the observed values.

5. Design an internal level assessment for your own bug backlog or representative task set. Describe what data collection, fitting and output tell you. Compare with METR data.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| METR | "External evaluator" | ex-ARC Evals; independent 501(c)(3) since Dec 2023 |
| Time Horizon | "Capability measure" | Expert task length at 50% reliability, from logistic fit |
| HCAST | "METR's main suite" | 180+ tasks spanning 1 min to 8+ hours |
| RE-Bench | "Research engineering" | 71 ML research-engineering tasks with human baseline |
| SWAA | "Short-task suite" | Calibrates the low end of the horizon curve |

| Upper bound | "Horizon is a ceiling" | Benchmark horizon > deployment reliability under load |
