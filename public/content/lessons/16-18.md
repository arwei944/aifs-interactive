# Psychological Theory and Emergency Coordination

> Li et al. (arXiv:2310.10701) demonstrated that LLM agents exhibit "emergent higher-order theory of mind" (ToM) in cooperative text games - reasoning about another agent's views on the beliefs of a third agent - but fail in long-term planning due to context management and hallucinations. Riedl (arXiv:2510.05174) measured the high-order synergy of the entire group and found that only tom prompt conditions would produce idential-related differentiation and goal-oriented complementarity. A less competent master of laws only shows a false appearance. That is to say, the emergence of coordination is conditional and model-dependent, rather than free. This lesson implements a minimal TOM-aware agent to run collaborative tasks with and without ToM prompts, and measures the coordination increment according to the Riedl 2025 protocol.

## "Problem"

Multi-agent coordination often seems magical: agents divide tasks, predict each other, and avoid redundancy. Usually, this kind of "emergence" is the product of rapid engineering - someone tells the agent to "coordinate". Remove the prompt, remove the coordination.

Riedl's discovery in 2025 was even more rigorous: under controlled conditions, coordination only occurs when an agent is prompted to infer the thoughts of other agents. Even a powerful model will display coordination patterns that cannot be statistically controlled without ToM's prompt. This is important for production: The "multi-agent coordination" feature released by the team relies on being immediate and fragile.

This lesson regards ToM as a specific ability (reasoning about beliefs), constructs a minimal ToM perception agent, and measures what true coordination looks like and what immediate dressing looks like.

## Concept

### What does "Tom" mean?

Developmental psychology: A 3-year-old child believes that everyone's inner world is the same as theirs. A five-year-old child knows that others have different beliefs. A 7-year-old child reasoned about the belief (" She thinks I think the ball is under the cup "). They are zero-order, first-order and second-order TOMs.

For LLM agents, ToM orders are mapped to:

- ** Zero-order: ** No other models. The agent acts only based on his own observations.
- ** First stage ** : A model in which agents have mutual proxy beliefs. Alice believes in x.
- Second order: Recursive belief of the proxy model. Alice believes Bob believes x.

Li et al. 2023 discovered that first-order and second-order ToM appear in LLM agents in cooperative games, but degenerate with the horizon length and unreliable communication.

### In a nutshell, it's the Sally Anne test

The 1985 false belief test: Sally put A marble into Basket A and then left. Annie moved it into Basket b. Where will Sally watch when she comes back? A child with a first-order ToM said basket A (Sally's belief is different from reality). The child without a basket says basket B.

Master of Laws in the gpt-4 era passed the Saly-Anne test while posing. When the narrative is long, the scene changes multiple times, or the problem is expressed indirectly, they will fail. This is ToM's actual state in 2026 during his Master of Laws in Production.

### Riedl coordinate measurement

Riedl (arXiv:2510.05174) constructed a population size test: N agents, one cooperative objective, and variable prompt conditions. Measures

1. Identity-linked differentiation. Will agents develop stable role distinctions over time?
2. Purposeful complementarity. Do the behaviors of the agents complement each other (different sub-tasks) rather than repeat?
3. "High-level synergy." A statistical method used to measure whether a group has achieved goals that other groups cannot reach.

Result: Only under the condition indicated by ToM will all three indicators generate signals above the baseline. Without ToM's prompt, for medium-capacity models, the metric hovers near the chance. Large models show a certain degree of coordination without explicit ToM prompts, but the effect is less than that of explicit prompts.

### Illusion of harmony

In the absence of statistical controls, "emergency coordination" in a presentation typically reflects:

- The project prompts coordination (the system prompts "Work together").
- Observer bias (we see the patterns we expect).
- Select the successful run afterwards.

A production system that promotes "emergency coordination" in the absence of measurable signals should be regarded as marketing. Pre-claim measurement.

### A minimal TOM-aware agent

Structure

```
agent state:
  own_beliefs:    {facts the agent believes}
  other_models:   {other_agent_id -> {beliefs_the_agent_attributes_to_them}}
  actions_last_N: [history of others' actions]

observation update:
  - update own_beliefs from direct observation
  - update other_models[agent_id] from their action + prior beliefs

action selection:
  - enumerate candidate actions
  - for each, predict what each other agent will do next given their modeled beliefs
  - pick action that maximizes joint outcome under those predictions
```

### Why does long-term investment cause harm?

Li et al. 's literature: Contextual limitations cause agents to forget which belief belongs to whom. Hallucinations add false beliefs to the other-model. Both will generate the error of "I thought he thought X", and over time, these errors will gradually intensify.

The mitigation measures recorded in the document and the follow-up actions for 2024-2026:

- Display the ToM status in the prompt. Structured format: Forced retrieval to maintain identity-belief binding.
- A shorter reasoning chain. Fewer ToM updates per round reduce compound hallucinations.
- External ToM storage. Maintain the model outside the LLM environment; Only inject the relevant components in each round.

### The places where ToM failed in production

- Hostile setup. Agents with good ToM are easier to operate (you can imitate the models they build for you and then take advantage of them).
- Heterogeneous teams. When the models are different, the ToM model applicable to one opponent cannot be generalized.
- Ground-truth dependent mission. ToM is about belief. If correctness depends on the facts, ToM might be distracting.

### The coordinates you can measure

Three practical signals indicate that the team's coordination is genuine rather than hasty:

1. Complementarity over time. In multi-round tasks, does the agent's action cover disjoint subtasks?
2. Looking forward to it. Does the behavior of actor A in the T+1 round depend on the correct prediction of B's behavior in the T+2 round?
3. "Corrected. When A misunderstood B's belief in Round T, did A correct it in round T+2?

These are measurable in the recorded multi-agent system. They are substantive versions of the "coordinated" narrative.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Theory of Mind | "Understanding others' minds" | The capacity to model another agent's beliefs. Graded by order (0, 1, 2+). |
| Sally-Anne test | "The false-belief test" | 1985 developmental psychology; LLMs pass plain versions, fail complex ones. |
| First-order ToM | "A believes X" | Modeling one other's beliefs about facts. |
| Second-order ToM | "A believes B believes X" | Recursive modeling one level deeper. |
| Identity-linked differentiation | "Stable roles over time" | Riedl's metric: roles persist, not random. |
| Goal-directed complementarity | "Disjoint actions" | Agents target different subtasks, not the same one. |
| Higher-order synergy | "Group exceeds any subset" | Riedl's statistical measure for real coordination. |
| Coordination illusion | "It looks coordinated" | Prompt-dressed appearance of coordination without measurable signal. |
