## This question

DeepSeek V3 is the first cutting-edge open model, and its architecture is quite different from that of the Llama series. Alpaca 3405b is "GPT-2 with six knobs for rotation." DeepSeek-V3 is GPT-2, with all six knobs plus four. Reading the Llama 3 configuration is a warm-up for reading the DeepSeek configuration, but the deep structure - the shape of the attention blocks, routing logic, and training time goals - is very different, so you need to practice it separately.

The rewards of learning it: The open weight release of DeepSeek V3 has changed the meaning of "frontier capabilities" in open models. This architecture is the blueprint that many training programs in 2026 are replicating. Understanding it is crucial for any role involving cutting-edge master of Laws training or reasoning.

## This concept

### Unchanging core

DeepSeek-V3 is still autoregressive. It will still stack decoder blocks. Each block still has attention plus MLP plus two rms specifications. It still uses SwiGLU in MLP. It still uses RoPE. Pre-norm. Weight-tied embedding. The baselines are the same as those of alpacas and northwest winds.

### Change: Replace GQA with MLA

From stage 10.14, you know that GQA reduces the KV cache by sharing K and V in groups of Q heads. The multi-head latent attention (MLA) goes a step further: K and V are compressed into a shared low-rank latent representation (MLA). The KV cache only stores latent - typically 512 floating-point numbers per layer per token, rather than 8 × 128 = 1024 floating-point numbers.

In a 128k environment, DeepSeek V3 and MLA (a shared potential)

```
kv_cache = num_layers * kv_lora_rank * max_seq_len * bytes_per_element
         = 61 * 512 * 131072 * 2
         = 7.6 GB
```

The assumed GQA baseline (alpaca 370B shape, 8 KV head, head luminance 128) will pay:

```
kv_cache = 2 * 61 * 8 * 128 * 131072 * 2
         = 30.5 GB
```

MLA is four times smaller than LamA-3-70B-style GQA cache in a 128k context.

Trade-off: MLA adds a decompression step for each attention calculation (each head). Compared with the saved bandwidth, the additional computing is very small. A net victory in long context reasoning.

### Routing: Auxiliary - Lossless load balancing

The MoE router determines which k top experts handle each token. A naive router concentrates too much work on a few experts, leaving others idle. Standard correction: Add an auxiliary loss term to penalize load imbalance. This is effective, but it will slightly reduce the performance of the main tasks.

DeepSeek-V3 introduces an auxiliary lossless scheme. The bias term of each expert is added to the router logits and adjusted during training through a simple rule: if the expert's load is insufficient, it is increased. No additional loss clause. Keep the training clean. The expert load remains balanced.

Impact on major losses: Unmeasurable. Impact on the MoE architecture: Cleaner, no auxiliary loss, and adjustable hyperparameters.

### MTP: Intensive Training + Free draft

From stage 10·18, you know that DeepSeek-V3 has added a D=1 MTP module, which can predict the tokens at the first two positions. During reasoning, the training module is reused as a speculative decoding draft with an acceptance rate of over 80%. During training, each hidden state is supervised on D+1 = 2 targets, thereby providing a denser signal.

Parameter: 671B on 14B. Expenditure :2.1%.

### Training: DualPipe

Starting from Phase 10.19, you know that DualPipe is a bidirectional pipe that overlaps all communications across nodes with the forward and backward blocks. At the 2,048-H800 scale of DeepSeeking -V3, it recovered approximately 245k gpu hours, while 1F1B might have suffered losses due to pipeline bubbles.

### Accounting of parameters

The complete calculation is in the title:

- Embedded
- The first three dense blocks: Pay attention to MLA (each block ~144M) + dense MLP (each block ~260M) + specification. It's approximately 1.2 billion in total.
- 58 MoE blocks: Follow MLA (~144M) + 256 experts (each 30M) + 1 shared expert (30M) + norm. The total is approximately 7.95B per piece, including all experts. There are 58 MoE blocks totaling 461B.
- MTP module: 14B.

Overall: The 476B + core architecture and the 14B + MTP. Clearly, the published 671B figure includes additional structural parameters (bias tensor, expert-specific components, shared expert scaling, etc.). The numbers we copied in the calculator are within the published 3-5% - the increments come from the fine-grained accounting of the DeepSeek report document, as shown in Section 2 of its appendix.

Parameters for each forwarding activity:

- Note: Each floor is 144M * 61 = 8.8B (fire on all floors).
- MLP activity: The first 3 layers are dense (3 * 260M = 780M), 58 MoE layers are active each layer, 8 routes + 1 sharing + route overhead. Effective MLP per layer: ~260M. Total: 3 * 260M + 58 * 260M = approximately 15.9B.
- Embedding + Specification: 1.2B.
- Total activity: approximately 26B cores + 14B MTP (training but not always running during inference) ≈37B.

### 671B / 37B ratio

A sparsity ratio of 18 times (active parameters account for 5.5% of the total). DeepSeek-V3 is the sparsest frontier MoE model that has been delivered with open weights. The mixed color 8x7B with a ratio of 13/47 (28%) has a greater density. The ratio of alpaca No. 4 calves is 17B/400B (4.25%). DeepSeek's bet is: at the frontier scale, more experts, a lower activation rate, and the quality of each activation flop will be better.

### Next, R1, V4

DeepSeek-R1 (2025) is an inference training run on the V3 backbone. R1 uses the same architecture. What has changed is the post-training formula (large-scale reinforcement learning on verifiable tasks), rather than the pre-training architecture.

DeepSeek v4 (if released) is expected to retain MLA + MoE + MTP and add DSA (DeepSeek Sparse Attention), which is the successor to NSA in stage 10.17. The spectrum is stable: architecture-level innovations are constantly accumulating; Each version has additional knobs.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| MLA | "Multi-Head Latent Attention" | Compress K and V into a shared low-rank latent (kv_lora_rank, typically 512), decompress per head on-the-fly; KV cache stores only the latent |
| kv_lora_rank | "MLA compression dim" | The size of the shared latent for K and V; DeepSeek-V3 uses 512 |
| First k dense layers | "Early layers stay dense" | The first few MoE-model layers skip the MoE router and run a dense MLP for stability |
| num_experts_per_tok | "Top-k routing" | How many routed experts fire per token; DeepSeek-V3 uses 8 |
| Shared experts | "Always-on experts" | Experts that process every token regardless of routing; DeepSeek-V3 uses 1 |
| Auxiliary-loss-free routing | "Bias-adjusted load balance" | Per-expert bias terms adjusted during training to keep expert load balanced without adding a loss term |
| MTP module | "Extra prediction head" | Transformer block predicting t+2 from h^(1) and E(t+1); denser training, free speculative-decoding draft |
| DualPipe | "Bidirectional pipeline" | Training schedule that overlaps forward/backward compute with cross-node all-to-all |
| Active parameter ratio | "Sparsity" | active_params / total_params; DeepSeek-V3 hits 5.5% |
| FP8 training | "8-bit training" | Training storage and many compute ops in FP8; roughly halves memory vs BF16 at a small quality cost |
