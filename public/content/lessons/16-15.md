# Voting, self-consistency and debate topology

> The cheapest aggregation: Sample N independent agents, majority voting. Wang et al. 2022 Self-consistency achieved this by sampling N times with one model. Multi-agent extends it by using heterogeneous agents to evade a single culture - different models, different prompts, different temperatures, and different contexts. In addition to majority voting, the debate topology is also important: MultiAgentBench (arXiv:2503.01935, ACL 2025) evaluated star/chain/tree/graph coordination and found that ** graphs are the most suitable for research **, with "coordination taxes" exceeding four agents. AgentVerse (ICLR 2024) documents two emergent patterns - volunteer behavior and conformity behavior - conformity is both a characteristic (seeking consensus) and a risk (Lesson 24, group thinking). This lesson maps topological Spaces, constructs each variant, and measures the coordination tax.

## "Problem"

Debate can improve accuracy (Du et al., arXiv:2305.14325). It can also degrade it. Whether a debate is helpful depends on four structural choices:

1. Who talks to whom (topology).
2. How many rounds (Du 2023: Both the rounds and the agents are independent).
3. Whether the agent is heterogeneous (different basic models break the single culture).
4. Are there any hostile voices (steelworkers vs. straw workers)?

Compared with a single agent, teams that "run five agents and vote" usually regress. Failure is not random. They track topological structure and heterogeneity. This lesson is about topological diagrams.

## Concept

### Self-consistency, single model baseline

Wang et al. 2022 (" Self-Consistency Improvement of Chain of Thought Reasoning ") sampled the same model N times at a temperature of b> and conducted majority voting on the answers of the reasoning paths. The result on GSM8K is that in a single greedy decoding, N=40 samples achieved considerable gains. Self-consistency is the predecessor of single-agent voting in multi-agent voting.

Limitation: Self-consistency uses a basic model. Errors are associated by structure. If the model has systematic bias, then all N samples will have systematic bias.

### Multi-agent voting, heterogeneous expansion

Replace N samples with N different agents. Different basic models (Claude, GPT, Llama), different prompts, and different tool accesses. The advantage is: irrelevant errors. Cost: Different chemicals have different costs. Coordinating them will increase expenses.

The normative name for heterogeneous debate in 2026 is **A-HMAD** - Adversarial Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Debate. Although not widely adopted, the paper uses the term "different modes of debate, which reduces the related errors caused by the collapse of a single culture."

### Four topologies

```
star                chain               tree                graph

    ┌─A─┐           A─B─C─D         ┌──A──┐              A───B
    │   │                           │     │              │ × │
    B   C                           B     C              D───C
    │   │                          / \   / \
    D   E                         D   E F   G           (fully connected)
```

Star: A hub, and everyone else only speaks to the hub. It's equivalent to a supervisor - worker without a reverse channel.
Chain: Linear, where each agent sees the output of the previous agent. Pipeline-like.
Tree: Hierarchical, used by hierarchical proxy systems (Lesson 06).

### Coordinated Tax (MultiAgentBench

MultiAgentBench (MARBLE, ACL 2025, arXiv:2503.01935) benchmarked star, chain, tree, and graph types on a task suite including research, coding, and planning. Main measurement results:

- The topology of the graph emerged victorious in the research task. Information flows from anyone to anyone. Agents can criticize each other.
- ** ** wins in the quick answer factual task. Hub filtering and merging.
- The "chain" emerged victorious in the step-by-step pipeline (phased optimization).
- The ** Coordination Tax ** shows the past ~4 agents in the graph topology. The growth rate of wall clock and token costs is faster than that of quality.

The upper limit of four agents is empirical rather than fundamental. It reflects the 2026 LLM context capacity: each agent's context is filled with the output of its peers. When everyone can see each person, the marginal value of adding agent N+1 will decrease.

### Multi-agent debate strategy (" Should we go crazy? ") )

arXiv:2311.17371 is the survey of the MAD strategy in 2023. A key finding repeated by others: When the same budget is used, MAD variants that are "structurally similar" to self-consistency (independent sampling + aggregation) typically perform poorly. MAD is most helpful when the agent is truly heterogeneous and the debate has an adversarial structure (one agent opposes).

### AgentVerse Emergency Mode

AgentVerse (ICLR 2024) https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/578e65cdee35d00c708d4c64bce32971-Paper-Conference.pdf) recorded the two from multi-agent The behaviors that arise in an argument, even without a clear design:

- "Volunteer. The agent will offer assistance proactively (" I can take the next step "). Useful: It assigns work to sub-tasks to the most capable agent.
- * *. The agent adjusts its posture to match the commenter, even if the commenter is wrong. This is flattery in debate (Lesson 14).

Consistency is why "debating until reaching an agreement" rewards bullies. A limited number of rounds conducted by a single judge.

### Heterogeneity: The movement accuracy of the actual knob

The 2024-2026 model in practical literature: Swapping one of N agents to a different base model brings a greater improvement in accuracy than increasing N by 1. Intuition is a single culture - every new independent source of error is more valuable than an additional relevant sample.

In extreme cases, heterogeneity trumps quantity. In most tasks, three different models are better than five copies of one model.

### The jury method

The Sibyl framework (cited in the Minsky-LLM literature) formally defines a "jury" - a group of specialized agents who improve the answers by voting at each stage. Unlike a simple majority vote, the jury has its own role: one agent cross-checks, one provides background, and one assesses reasonableness. The jury's approach lies at the midpoint between ordinary voting (cheap, tending towards a single culture) and complete MAD (expensive, tending towards conformity).

### When the debate and voting are dominant

- This problem has basic facts (facts, mathematics, code behavior). Voting convergence makes sense.
- The agent can access different sources or tools (heterogeneity is available).
- The rounds are limited (typically 2-3 rounds), and there is a separate referee or validator.
- The budget allows for 3 to 5 agents. In Figure Topology 5-7 and above, the harmonized tax is dominant.

### When debating voting is harmed

- This issue is determined by the viewpoint. The agent converges to the answer that seems the most confident rather than the most correct one.
- All agents share a basic model. A single culture makes consensus meaningless.
- A circle is infinite. Following the rules always leads to victory.
- The task is very simple. A single agent with self-consistency at N=5 is cheaper and equally accurate.

## This ship

For any combination:

- Starting from the self-consistency when N=5, use a strong foundation model. This is a cheap benchmark.
- If accuracy is important, upgrade to heterogeneous voting at N=3. Measure delta.
- Upgrade to the ** debate topology ** only when the task has a structure (research, multi-step) and bounded rounds are feasible.
- Always record minority clusters. When a few people are always right, you get a signal of diversity.
- Benchmark wall clocks, tokens and accuracy. "Improving accuracy at ten times the cost" is a business decision.

## Practice

1. Run the coordinated tax curve of the graph topology: precision vs N, token vs N. At what N points does the curve bend?
2. Implement A-HMAD: Three agents with intentionally different biases. In the single-culture attack of Lesson 14, how does the congruence bias baseline compare with A-HMAD?
3. Add a "judge" role to the graph topology. This role does not vote but only scores the final consensus. Will this change the behavior of emergency conformity?
4. Read the AgentVerse paper (ICLR 2024). Determine that your implementation exhibits the most intense urgent behavior. Can you trigger the opposite behavior through a rapid change?
5. Read Section 4 (Topological Experiments) of MultiAgentBench (arXiv:2503.01935). Use your tools to reproduce the results of "charts outweigh research" on a task in the paper.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Self-consistency | "Sample N times, vote" | Wang 2022. Single model, N temperature>0 samples, majority vote on reasoning paths. |
| Heterogeneity | "Different models" | Ensemble of different base models or prompt families. Breaks monoculture. |
| MAD | "Multi-agent debate" | Generic term for agents exchanging critiques over rounds. See Du 2023. |
| A-HMAD | "Adversarial Heterogeneous MAD" | MAD variant emphasizing different models + adversarial structure. |
| Topology | "Who talks to whom" | Star, chain, tree, graph. Determines information flow. |
| Coordination tax | "Diminishing returns" | Above ~4 agents on graph, cost grows faster than quality. |
| Volunteer behavior | "Unprompted help" | AgentVerse emergent pattern: an agent offers to take a step. |
| Conformity behavior | "Agreement under pressure" | AgentVerse emergent pattern: an agent aligns with a critic. |
| Jury | "Small specialized panel" | Sibyl-style ensemble with roles (examiner, context, scorer). |
