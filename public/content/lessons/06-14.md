# Voice activity detection and rotation - Silver, Cobra, and flush tricks

> The life and death of each voice agent depends on two decisions: Is the user speaking now? VAD answered the first question. Turning detection (VAD + Silent hangover + Semantic Endpoint model) answered the second question. No matter which one goes wrong, your assistant will either cut off the user's contact or never shut up.

**Type:** Build
**Languages:** Python
**Prerequisites:** Phase 6 · 11 (Real-Time Audio), Phase 6 · 12 (Voice Assistant)
**Time:** ~45 minutes

## This question

The voice agent makes three different decisions every 20 milliseconds:

1. Is this framework voice? ** -vad. Binary, per frame.
2. Has the user started a new discourse? - Start the detection.
3. Has the user completed? - End pointing (turning end).

The naive answer (energy threshold) does not apply to any noise - the hustle and bustle of traffic, keyboards, or crowds. The answer for 2026 is: Silero VAD (Open, Deep Learning) + turning detection models (semantic endpoints) + the silent hangover of VAD calibration.

## This concept


### Three-layer VAD cascading

*The first level: The Gate of Energy. "Cheap. The effective value of the threshold is -40 DBFS. Filter out obvious silence, but fire at any noise that exceeds the threshold.

**Tier 2: Silero VAD** (2020-2026, MIT) 1 m parameter. Received training in over 6,000 languages. On a single CPU thread, it runs for approximately 1 millisecond every 30-millisecond block. The TPR is 87.7% and the FPR is 5%. Open source default values.

*Layer 3: Semantic Shift Detector. LiveKit's turn detection model (2024-2026) or your own small classifier. Distinguish between "pauses in the sentence" and "finished speaking". Use the language context (intonation + new words), not just silence.

### Key parameters and their default values

- "Threshold. Silero outputs a probability; Classify the voice as &gt; 0.5 (default) or &gt; 0.3 (sensitive). A lower threshold = fewer first-word fragments and more false positives.
- Minimum speaking time. Reject voice messages shorter than 250 milliseconds - usually coughing or chair noise.
- "Aftereffects of silence (End direction)." After VAD returns to 0, wait for 500 to 800 ms to declare the end. Too short → Interrupt the user. Too long → Dull sensation.
- Opening buffer. Maintain an audio duration of 300 to 500 milliseconds before the VAD catches fire. Prevent "hey" from being cut off.

### Tonghuashun Tricks (Jiutai 2025)

The streaming STT model has a pre-check delay (500 ms for Kyutai STT-1B and 2.5 s for STT-2.6B). Usually, you have to wait for such a long time after your speech to get the draft. Refresh technique: When the VAD triggers the voice to end, ** send a refresh signal to the STT** to force immediate output. STT processes at four times the real-time speed, so a 500-millisecond buffer is completed within 125 milliseconds.

End-to-end: 125 ms VAD + flush STT = session latency.

### Step 4: the flush trick skeleton

```python
def flush_on_end(stt_client, audio_buffer):
    stt_client.send_audio(audio_buffer)
    stt_client.send_flush()
    return stt_client.recv_transcript(timeout_ms=150)
```

STT (Kyutai, Deepgram, AssemblyAI) must support flush to function. Whisper flow doesn't - it's block-based and always waits for blocks.

## Use it

| Situation | VAD choice |
|-----------|-----------|
| Open, fast, general | Silero VAD |
| Commercial call center | Cobra VAD |
| On-device (phone) | Silero VAD ONNX |
| Research / diarization | pyannote segmentation |
| Zero-dependency fallback | WebRTC VAD (legacy) |
| Need turn-ending quality | Silero + LiveKit turn-detector layered |

Rule of thumb: Never use a pure energy VAD unless you really have no other options.

## Trap

- "Fixed threshold. Work in silence and fail in noise. Calibrate the equipment or switch to Silero.
- The aftereffects of a too short silence. The operator interrupted in the middle. 500 to 800 milliseconds is the optimal point for conversational speech.
- A hangover that lasts too long. Dull sensation. Conduct A/B testing for the target users.
- No pre-roll buffer. The user's audio is lost for the first 200 to 300 milliseconds. Always keep the rolling pre-roll.
- Ignore semantic endpoints. "Well, let me think..." It contains very long pauses. Users hate being interrupted in the middle. Use LiveKit's turn detector or something similar.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|-----------------|-----------------------|
| VAD | Voice detector | Binary per-frame: is this speech? |
| Turn detection | End-pointing | VAD + silence-hangover + semantic endpoint. |
| Silence hangover | Wait-after-speech | Time to wait before declaring turn end; 500-800 ms. |
| Pre-roll | Pre-speech buffer | Keep 300-500 ms audio before VAD fires. |
| Flush trick | Kyutai hack | VAD → flush-STT → 125 ms instead of 500 ms delay. |
| Semantic endpoint | "Did they mean to stop?" | ML classifier that looks at words, not just silence. |
| TPR @ FPR 5% | ROC point | Standard VAD benchmark; 87.7% for Silero, 50% WebRTC. |
