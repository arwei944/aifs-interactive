# CAIS, CAISI and social-scale risks

> The Center for Artificial Intelligence Security (CAIS, San Francisco, founded by hendricks and Zhang in 2022) released four risk frameworks - malicious use, AI race, organizational risk, and rogue AI - as well as a statement on extinction risk signed by hundreds of professors and company leaders in May 2023. In 2026, CAIS will release: AI Dashboard for Frontier Model Evaluation, Remote Labor Index (with Scale AI), Super Intelligence Strategy Document, and AI Frontier Communications. A unique entity: the NIST Center for Artificial Intelligence Standards and Innovation (CAISI) - a voluntary protocol and non-confidential capability assessment for the U.S. government, with a focus on cyber, biological, and chemical weapons risks. CAIS marks organizational risk as one of the four top risks: security culture, strict auditing, multi-layered defense, and information security are the foundation, but they are usually weighed against deployment speed. If California's SB-53 is signed, it will become the first state-level catastrophe risk regulatory regulation in the United States.

## This question

Lessons 19 and 20 cover the internal scale policy of the laboratory. Lesson 21 covers the assessment of independent ability. This lesson covers the third perspective: civil society and government organizations, which shape the public discussion and regulatory baseline of catastrophic artificial intelligence risks.

Two different entities are very important. CAIS is a non-profit research organization that releases a thinking framework on the risks of artificial intelligence and coordinates public statements. CAISI is a U.S. government center within NIST that has reached voluntary agreements with laboratories and conducts non-confidential capability assessments. The name rhymes; These two tasks do not overlap. A practitioner should know both.

The four-risk framework of CAIS is the most widely cited classification of social-scale risks in the literature. Safety culture and organizational risk are among them, which are the ones that practitioners control most directly. If SB-53 (California) is signed, it will be the first state-level catastrophe risk regulatory law in the United States. The framework of this bill is very important because in terms of US science and technology policy, state-level regulation has traditionally dominated federal actions.

## This concept

### CAIS - Center for Artificial Intelligence Security

- Founded in 2022 by Dan hendricks and his colleagues in San Francisco (" Zhang "refers to an early collaborator, not the current co-founder; for current leadership, please refer to the CAIS website).
- Status: 501(c)(3) Non-profit.
- Notable 2023 achievement: A statement on extinction risks jointly signed by hundreds of researchers and ceos. He said, "Reducing the extinction risk posed by artificial intelligence should, along with other social-scale risks such as epidemics and nuclear war, become a global priority."
- Output in 2026: AI dashboards for cutting-edge model evaluation, remote labor index (in conjunction with large-scale AI), super-intelligence strategy documents, AI frontier communications.

### Four Risk Framework

The framework of CAIS divides catastrophic artificial intelligence risks into four top categories:

1. ** Malicious use ** : Bad actors use artificial intelligence to cause harm (biological weapon synthesis, false information, cyber attacks).
2. ** Artificial Intelligence Race ** : The competitive pressure among laboratories, companies or countries drives deployment beyond the safety point.
3. ** Organizational Risk ** : Internal laboratory dynamics (failure of security culture, insufficient auditing, and inadequate security resources) lead to poor deployment.
4. ** Rogue AI ** : An AI capable enough to pursue goals that conflict with human welfare.

This is not the only classification method; It is the most frequently cited. These categories are not mutually exclusive - rogue artificial intelligence produced by an organization that trades audits for speed in competitions is all four.

### Where organizational risks exist

Among these four categories, organizational risk is the most actionable for practitioners. The safety culture of the laboratory, the rigor of the audit, the defense layer and information security determine whether their model has 10-18 lessons of control or whether these controls are checklist items that have not been verified by anyone.

Specific organizational risk leverage

- ** Safety Culture ** : Can team members escalate problems without sacrificing their careers? CAIS 'investigation found that this is a powerful prediction for other levers.
- ** Strict auditing ** : external and internal. Internal audits produce optimistic reports.
- ** Multi-layer Defense ** : Single-layer defense is not sufficient (the running theme of stage 15).
- ** Information Security ** : Model weight leakage, eval data leakage, monitoring bypass technology leakage. The RAND SL-4 in Lesson 19 is a specific standard.

### Artificial Intelligence Standards and Innovation Center

- Run within NIST.
- Sign a voluntary agreement with the frontier laboratory.
- Publish non-confidential capability assessments for cyber, biological and chemical weapons risks.
- Different from CAIS; Acronyms collide with each other; Check the URL (nist.gov) to confirm which one you are reading.

The role of CAISI is the counterpart of the public, government-oriented METR's private laboratory (Lesson 21). CAISI's report is non-confidential. METR reports are usually confidential. A practitioner who reads both books simultaneously will gain a more comprehensive understanding.

### California SB-53

The California Senate Bill (2025-2026) addresses the catastrophic risks posed by cutting-edge models. The main proposed terms:

- The specific capability threshold for triggering state-level obligations.
- Whistleblower protection for employees of artificial intelligence laboratories.
- Event reporting requirements for catastrophic failures.

If signed, this will be the first state-level catastrophe risk regulatory regulation in the United States. Regardless of the signing status, the framework of the bill determines how other state legislators handle this issue. Practitioners in California should keep track of the status of the bill. Practitioners in other places should read this book to understand what state-level regulation in the United States might look like.

### The risk of social scale is not a single-level issue

The theme of stage 15 - defense in depth - is also applicable at the social level. No single organization, regulation or framework can shut down catastrophic risks. An ecosystem can only function under the following circumstances:

- Laboratory Ship Scaling Strategy (Lessons 19 and 20)
- External evaluators generate measurement results (Lesson 21).
- Civil Society Tracking and Advocacy (CAIS)
- The government operates voluntary programs and benchmark regulation (CAISI, SB-53).
- Practitioners Build Multi-layer Controls (Lessons 10-18).

This is the final summary of this stage: Each previous lesson is a layer in the stack, and its integrity is more important than the strength of any layer.

## Practice

1. Run and input three synthetic deployments of different scales. Confirm whether the four risk labels meet your expectations; Determine the situation where a tool has too few or too many marks.

2. Please read the four risk papers of CAIS in full. Choose a risk category and write two paragraphs to discuss what you think are the most important developments in this category in 2026.

How much does it cost to make it meet the standard?

5. Outline a four-risk framework for the 2028 version, reflecting one year of additional capabilities and one year of additional deployment experience. What will you add, delete or recombine?
