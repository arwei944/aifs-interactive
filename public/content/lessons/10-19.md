# DualPipe parallelism

> DeepSeek V3 is trained on 2048 H800 Gpus, with MoE experts scattered across various nodes. For each gpu hour of calculation, the communication cost of cross-node expert full-to-full communication is 1 gpu hour. The gpu is idle for half of the time. DualPipe (DeepSeek, December 2024) is a bidirectional pipe that computes forward and backward with all the communications they trigger overlapping. The bubble drops and the throughput rises. Once expert parallelism has spread experts at all levels, it becomes cheap to keep two copies of model parameters (the "double" in the name). This class is a learning-oriented explanation of what DualPipe actually does and why the DualPipeV improvement by Sea AI Lab reduced the cost of 2x parameters at the expense of slightly tighter bubbles.

**Type:** Learn
**Languages:** Python (stdlib, schedule simulator)
**Prerequisites:** Phase 10 · 05 (distributed training, FSDP, DeepSpeed), Phase 10 · 14 (open-model architectures and MoE)

## This question

Training the 671B MoE model on a 2k H800 gpu will encounter three compound bottlenecks:

1. "Memory pressure. Each GPU holds a slice of the model. The activation memory of 8k sequences on 61 layers of 128 magnetic heads is huge.
2. Pipe foam. The traditional pipeline parallelism (GPipe, 1F1B) keeps the gpu idle while waiting for the input or gradient of its stage. Among the 8 stages, even with 1F1B scheduling, approximately 12% of the GPU time can bubble.
3. All by Cross-node. MoE with expert parallelism distributes experts to various nodes. Each forward pass triggers an all-to-all token to be distributed to their expert, and another token is combined. On a 2k gpu, this can easily become a 1:1 ratio of computer to communication.

These problems all have their own solutions: gradient checkpoints for memory, Zero Bubble for pipeline bubbles (Sea AI Lab, 2023), and expert parallel communication kernel for everyone. What DualPipe does is to make them play together. Scheduling superimposes computing and communication in a forward and backward block, while injecting micro-batches from both ends of the pipeline and using the generated scheduling to hide everything in the computing window.

Report result: During the 14.8t token training run of DeepSeek V3, the pipeline bubble was almost eliminated, and the GPU utilization exceeded 95%.

## This concept

### Parallel refreshing of pipelines

Split the n-layer model on P devices. The device micro-batch flows forward through device 0 to P-1, and then flows backward from P-1 to 0. Each device can only start its forward phase when the previous device sends output, and can only start its backward phase when the downstream device sends the upstream gradient.

GPipe (Huang et al., 2019) schedules one micro-batch at a time, which wastes most of the GPU time. 1F1B (Narayanan et al., 2021) conducted front-to-back cross-transfer for multiple micro-batches. Zero Bubble (Qi et al., 2023) divides the reverse pass into two parts - the reverse input (B) and the reverse weight (W) - and schedules them to fill the bubble. After zero foam, the pipe is almost taut.

DualPipe is the next step. It added two ideas on top:

### Idea 1: Block decomposition

Each forwarding block is divided into four parts:

- "Attention. ** Q/K/V projection, note, output projection.
- All scheduling. Cross-node communication for sending tokens to experts.
- "Delay. MoE expert calculation.
- All combinations. Cross-node communication brings back expert output.

A gradient version that adds these elements to the rear block. DualPipe schedules them so that all dispatches to all are carried out in parallel with the attention calculation of the next block, and all combinations are carried out in parallel with the MLP calculation of the next block.

### Idea 2: Bidirectional scheduling

Most pipelines plan to inject micro-batches from level 0 and flow to level P-1. DualPipe injects micro-batches from both ends. Stage 0: See the micro-batches start there; Stage P-1 also saw micro-batches from there. Two small streams converge in the middle.

To make it work, this is the "dual" part of DualPipe: each device retains two copies of the model layer it needs to serve (one in each direction). At the scale of DeepSeek V3, this is twice the cost of parameter replication. It is affordable because expert parallelism has already thinly dispersed MoE experts, so replicating the non-expert layer twice is insignificant.

It is crucial that the forward flow in one direction and the backward flow in the other precisely overlap at the position of the bubble in a single direction. The bubble has disappeared.

### A hand-drawn schedule

Consider P = 4 grades, 8 micro-batches, divided into 4 forward / 4 reverse. Time moves from left to right. A row is the grade of the equipment.

```
           Time →
rank 0:  F1 F2 F3 F4  F5R F6R F7R F8R  B1 B2 B3 B4  ...
rank 1:     F1 F2 F3  F4/F5R F6R F7R   B1 B2 ...
rank 2:        F1 F2  F3/F5R F4/F6R    B1 ...
rank 3:           F1  F2/F5R F3/F6R    ...
```

Read the "F4/F5R" symbol: Within the same time period, micro-batch 4 (from left to right in the pipeline) and micro-batch 5 (from right to left), ranked 1, move forward. This is the operational meaning of "bidirectional".

The cross-flow overlaps faster at level 2, and the cross-flow overlaps the latest at levels 0 and P-1. During the stable intermediate stage of scheduling, each rank runs in the forward direction of the x direction and the backward direction of the y direction, overlapping. The computer is very busy. All forward passes for all schedules are hidden in backward computations. All pairs of all combinations are hidden in the forward computation. The bubbles have been squeezed out.

### Bubble accounting

Standard 1F1B pipe bubbles (time wasted for each grade) :

```
bubble_1F1B = (P - 1) * forward_chunk_time
```

The Zero Bubble is improved to reduce it, but not to zero. If the number of micro-batches can be divided evenly by twice the depth of the pipe, then DualPipe has zero bubbles in the stable stage. Outside the stable stage (preheating and cooling), there are some bubbles, but they do not increase with the number of micro-batches - this is a key feature emphasized in this article.

In marketing terms: "bubble-free". In technical terms: Bubbles do not grow with the micro-batch count. Subsequent analysis (DualPipeV/Cut-in-half) by Sea AI Lab shows that complete zero bubbles occur only when Expert Parallelism is not a bottleneck; There are always some scheduling compromises when using EP-driven all-to-all.

### DualPipeV - Refinement

Sea AI Lab (2025) observed that when EP communication overlap is not the focus, 2x parameter replication is wasted. Their DualPipeV scheduling folds bidirectional injection into a "V-shaped" schedule that runs on a single parameter copy. The bubble is slightly larger than DualPipe, but the memory saved is considerable. DeepSeek adopted DualPipeV as the EP-off mode in their open-source DualPipe implementation.

Trade-off

| Feature | DualPipe | DualPipeV | 1F1B | Zero Bubble |
|---------|---------|-----------|------|------------|
| Param copies per device | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Bubble vs micro-batches | constant | small growth | grows | grows |
| Compute-comm overlap | full | partial | minimal | partial |
| Use when | EP-heavy MoE | dense or EP-light | baseline | any pipeline |

### What does this mean for the operation of the 14.8t token

The pre-training of DeepSeek V3 consumed 14.8T of tokens on 2,048 H800 Gpus within approximately 2.8 million gpu hours. Using the naive 1F1B, they will lose 12-15% of the pipe bubbles - 340-420K gpu hours, which is enough to train a complete 70B model. DualPipe has restored most of it. Without internal logs, it is difficult to directly quantify contributions, but the paper claims that the average utilization rate of Gpus throughout the training process exceeded 95%.

For smaller runs (below 1k gpu), DualPipe is redundant - pipe bubbles are relatively small relative to the total cost, and intensive model training rarely encounters all the bottlenecks. For the training of boundary MoE on the scale of thousands of Gpus, it needs to be carried out effectively.

### Its position in the stack

- Supplement **FSDP** (Issue 10.05) FSDP performs cross-order sharding of model parameters; DualPipe cross-level scheduling computation. They got together.
- Compatible with **ZeRO-3** gradient sharding. The bookkeeping of dual-replica replication needs to be coordinated with ZeRO's sharding gradient.
- Custom full-to-full-kernel tuning is required for specific cluster topologies. The open-source kernel of DeepSeek is a reference implementation.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Pipeline bubble | "Idle time per rank" | GPU cycles wasted because a pipeline stage is waiting for its input or gradient |
| 1F1B | "Default pipeline schedule" | One forward / one backward interleaved scheduling; the baseline DualPipe beats |
| Zero Bubble | "Sea AI Lab 2023" | Splits backward into B (input gradient) and W (weight gradient); almost fully tightens the pipeline |
| DualPipe | "DeepSeek-V3 schedule" | Bidirectional pipeline + compute-comm overlap; bubbles do not grow with micro-batch count |
| DualPipeV | "Cut-in-half" | V-shape refinement that drops the 2x parameter replication at the cost of slightly larger bubbles |
| Chunk | "Unit of pipeline work" | A forward or backward pass of one micro-batch through one pipeline stage |
| All-to-all dispatch | "Send tokens to experts" | Cross-node comm that routes tokens to their assigned MoE experts |
| All-to-all combine | "Bring expert outputs back" | Cross-node comm that gathers expert outputs after the MLP |
| Expert Parallelism (EP) | "Experts across GPUs" | Shards MoE experts across ranks so different GPUs hold different experts |
| Pipeline Parallelism (PP) | "Layers across GPUs" | Shards model layers across ranks; the dimension DualPipe schedules |
| Bubble fraction | "Wasted GPU time" | (bubble_time / total_time); the fraction DualPipe drives toward zero |
