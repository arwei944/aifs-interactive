# The Debate on mental Society and multi-agent

> The premise proposed by Minsky in 1986 - that intelligence is a society composed of experts - is rediscovered every ten years. In 2023, Du et al. transformed it into a concrete algorithm: multiple LLM instances propose answers, read each other's answers, criticize and update. After N rounds of testing, they finally reached a consensus that defeated zero-shot CoT and reflections on six reasoning and factual tasks. Two findings are important: both "multi-agent" and "multi-round" contribute independently. Society has triumphed over the monologue of a single agent; Multiple rounds of exchanges are better than one vote.

## "Problem"

Self-consistency - sampling a model multiple times and then taking the majority of the answers - is the cheapest inference improvement you can rely on. It was effective, but it soon became saturated. You can double the sample without seeing another meaningful jump.

The debate broke the saturation. Instead of obtaining N independent samples from one model, N agents read each other's reasoning and make modifications. The correlation between samples declines (they are no longer i.i.d), and where i.i.d voting is definitely wrong, convergence points are usually correct.

## Concept

### Du et al. 2023 algorithm

From arXiv:2305.14325 (ICML 2024) :

1. Each of the N agents will generate the initial answer to the question.
2. For the circle r = 2... R: Each agent displays the R-1 round answers of other agents and is asked to "take these into account and provide your latest answer."
3. After Round R, the majority voted to determine the final answer.

The paper tested MMLU, GSM8K, biography, mathematics and factual benchmarks. Debate always prevails over CoT and self-reflection.

### Two independent knobs

Melting on the same sheet of paper

- ** Single agent count ** (1 round, majority vote of N) defeated single agent in most tasks, but stagnated.
- The number of rounds in a single round (where one agent sees its prior reasoning) is of little help - a known weakness of reflection.
- The two together make a big jump. Multi-round switching drive gain among multiple agents.

### Why does it work?

Two mechanisms:

1. "Contact differences. When an agent sees that another agent's reasoning chain has different conclusions, it must prove or update. In any case, the context of r+1 is richer than that of r.
2. Reduce relevant errors. In self-consistency, all samples come from the same model, so the errors are correlated - on average, you get a confident wrong answer. Different models or different seeds are not related. Different "controversial viewpoints" further disconnect the relevance.

### Heterogeneous debate

A-HMAD and related follow-ups use different basic models for different drugs. The Llama + Claude + GPT debate reduces the collapse of a single culture (Lesson 26), as the relevant mistakes of a model family are not shared by other families.

Disadvantage: The weak model involved in the debate may drag the consensus towards the wrong answer (see "Should We Go Crazy?") (arXiv:2311.17371)

### NLSOM - 129 proxy extensions

Zhuge et al. (Thought Storms in Psychosocial Society Based on Natural Language, arXiv:2305.17066) extended this idea to a society of 129 members. The result is that specialization and self-organization emerge as the scale expands, and the system performs better than a single agent in tasks such as visual question answering.

### Failure mode

- Flattery cascade. All agents listen to the one who sounds most confident. The debate was interrupted by the loudest voice. It is helpful to prompt adversarial roles (" an agent must assert the opposite position ").
- "Theme drift. Many debates have deviated from the original issue. Mitigation method: Re-inject the problem in each round.
- The calculation crashed. N agents × R rounds = N·R LLM calls, each of which has a growing context. A five-person, five-round debate was 25 decisions made against a backdrop of continuous development. The cost of each issue may exceed ten times that of a single CoT call.

## This ship

If you post a debate:

- The cap is capped at three rounds. Du et al. demonstrated capturing most of the gain in three rounds. It's more about cost than quality.
- The hat agent is in 5 When it exceeds level 5, environmental inflation and costs take the lead.
- The default is heterogeneous. There are at least two different basic models in the pool.
- Hostile slots. An agent was prompted to disagree. Flattery.
- Record each round. The debate system that hides the intermediate round cannot be debugged or audited.

## Practice

1. At which round does the operation of additional convergence stop?
2. Add a fourth antagonistic agent: always disagree with the current majority. Will this undermine or promote convergence?
3. Draw (print) the protocol score for each round (representing the percentage of the majority of answers). When did it reach 1.0, and is this equivalent to "correct"?
4. Read Du et al. Section Four Ablation. Use this code to copy the results of "Proxy only", "Poll only", and "both".
5. Read "Should We Go Crazy?" (arXiv:2311.17371) List two forms of debate that go beyond the round-robin stage - for example, judge-led, debate chain, and confrontational.

## Key terms

| Term | What people say | What it actually means |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Society of Mind | "Minsky's idea" | Intelligence as interacting specialists; 1986 framing now operationalized via LLM debate. |
| Multi-agent debate | "Agents argue" | N agents propose, critique each other, revise over R rounds, majority-vote. |
| Consensus | "They agree" | Not epistemic truth — just fraction-on-majority-answer. Can be confidently wrong. |
| Rounds | "Exchange steps" | One round = each agent reads the others and updates once. |
| Heterogeneous debate | "Mix model families" | Using different base models to decorrelate errors. |
| Sycophancy cascade | "Everyone agrees with the loud one" | Debate failure where agents defer to the most confident agent regardless of correctness. |
| NLSOM | "129-agent society" | Natural-language society of mind; Zhuge et al.'s scaled version. |
| Correlated error | "Same model, same bug" | Why self-consistency saturates; debate across different views decorrelates. |
